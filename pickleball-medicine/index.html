Return to play

My calf feels fine in doubles. Am I ready for a tournament?

One game with familiar partners can feel very different from several matches in a day. After a calf injury, I want to know what happens when you have to push off fast, do it again, then come back for the next match.

Which shot still changes your footwork?

Reaching for a wide dink, moving up to the kitchen and turning to chase a lob each ask something different of the calf. Knowing which movement you avoid helps focus the exam.

How much play have you tolerated?

A short drill, one doubles game and a draw with several matches are different workloads. Match length, time between games and what happens as you tire all matter.

How do you feel the next morning?

Soreness, stiffness or a limp after play can change the plan. A sudden pop or a new inability to push off warrants prompt assessment for a possible Achilles injury.

Published research

Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine · 2026

Injuries on the professional pickleball tour

Kimberly Burbank and I studied 75 publicly documented injuries among elite players. Nearly half involved the lower body. We looked at time away from competition, a question players at every level bring to the clinic. An individual player’s recovery still depends on the specific injury.

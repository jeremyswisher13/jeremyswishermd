Before you start

For adult baseball or softball players and other overhand throwers with a clinician-confirmed diagnosis of rotator cuff-related shoulder pain. You have completed the starter shoulder program or equivalent rehabilitation, and your treating clinician agrees you can begin a throwing progression.

This program may fit

Reaching, dressing and sleeping are comfortable. You can move through the throwing range you normally use without pain or a feeling of slipping. Your clinician has checked shoulder strength, repeated effort and control of your arm and trunk.

Get assessed first

A recent fall, collision or painful pop, a suspected full-thickness tear, previous dislocation with ongoing instability, or pain or tingling traveling down the arm needs assessment. Children and teens, athletes recovering from surgery and windmill pitchers need a separate plan.

Stop signs

Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting. Go to the emergency department now if your shoulder looks out of place or your hand is cold, pale or blue. A newly hot, swollen and severely painful shoulder also needs emergency department care now, even without fever or chills. Seek same-day urgent assessment for sudden weakness or inability to raise the arm after injury, or new persistent numbness.

Check the next morning

Throwing should be pain-free. Keep track of soreness after the session, night waking and any loss of motion. Check again the next morning. Include warm-ups, practice and recreational throws in your daily total.

Your home program

Build from shoulder control to easy catch

Choose drills for your stage with your treating clinician. The strength and ball-free drills below give examples of starting doses. Before catch, agree on your total throw count and distance with your clinician. Warm up with a brisk walk and comfortable arm movements. Count every warm-up throw toward your total.

Frequency
Strength on 2 nonconsecutive days a week. Leave at least one full day without throwing between early catch sessions.
Equipment
Light resistance band, towel, stable wall, regulation ball, throwing partner and level open space
First checkpoint
Record total throws (including warm-ups), distance and effort. Note how long soreness lasts after throwing and whether comfort and motion feel usual the next morning.
Primary goal
Complete a full practice in your position with pain-free throwing and usual comfort and motion the next morning. Decide about competition with your treating clinician and team.
  1. 01
    Exercise 1

    Band rotation with elbow at your side

    2 nonconsecutive days a week

    DoseA starting example is 1 to 2 sets of 8 to 12 slow repetitions.

    Secure a light band at elbow height on the side opposite the working arm. Bend the elbow and tuck a towel between it and your ribs. Keeping the towel still, turn your forearm outward, then control the return.

    Make it easier

    Step closer to the anchor or use a lighter band.

    Progress it

    Increase resistance a little once the full set stays smooth and your shoulder feels usual the next morning.

    My starting dose or notes

  2. 02
    Exercise 2

    Wall push-up with a forward reach

    2 nonconsecutive days a week

    DoseA starting example is 1 to 2 sets of 8 to 12 repetitions.

    Place your hands on a wall at shoulder height. Bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the wall. Straighten them, then gently push the wall away a little farther so your shoulder blades slide around your ribs. Keep your neck relaxed.

    Make it easier

    Stand closer to the wall or shorten the lowering movement.

    Progress it

    Move to a stable countertop once the wall version stays comfortable and controlled.

    My starting dose or notes

  3. 03
    Exercise 3

    Step and turn without a ball

    During throwing preparation

    DoseA starting example is 1 set of 5 slow rehearsals. Stop sooner if your movement changes.

    Stand sideways to your partner. Step toward them with the foot opposite your throwing arm. Let your hips and chest turn as your arm follows slowly through a comfortable throwing motion. Finish balanced on the lead leg.

    Make it easier

    Shorten the step and use a smaller arm movement.

    Progress it

    Link the step, turn and follow-through smoothly while keeping the rehearsal relaxed.

    My starting dose or notes

  4. 04
    Exercise 4

    Short, easy catch on flat ground

    After your clinician approves starting, with at least one full day off throwing between early sessions

    DoseUse the total throw count and distance you agreed on with your treating clinician. Include every warm-up throw in that total.

    At your agreed distance, throw with an easy arc on level ground. Step toward your partner, follow through and pause between throws. Stop at the planned total. Stop sooner for shoulder or elbow pain, loss of control or fatigue that changes how you throw.

    Make it easier

    If you have to force the throw at the agreed distance, stop. Review a shorter distance with your treating clinician before your next session.

    Progress it

    After repeated pain-free sessions with usual comfort and motion the next morning, change one workload variable at a time with your clinician. Before throwing harder, have your clinician check that you still control your arm, trunk and follow-through at the current workload.

    My starting dose or notes

Symptom response

Let the next day guide the dose.

Throwing should be pain-free. Keep track of soreness after the session, night waking and any loss of motion. Check again the next morning. Include warm-ups, practice and recreational throws in your daily total.

Continue

Green light

You throw without shoulder or elbow pain, keep your usual arm action and stay within the planned count and distance. Comfort and motion feel usual afterward and the next morning. Repeat this workload before increasing it.

Adjust

Yellow light

Stop throwing for the day if your shoulder or elbow hurts, your arm feels unusually heavy, or your arm action or accuracy changes. If soreness lasts more than 1 hour after throwing or is present the next morning, take at least one full day off throwing. Once comfort and motion are back to usual, repeat the last comfortable workload. Contact your treating clinician if soreness recurs.

Stop

Red light

Stop throwing. Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting. Go to the emergency department now if your shoulder looks out of place or your hand is cold, pale or blue. A newly hot, swollen and severely painful shoulder also needs emergency department care now, even without fever or chills. Seek same-day urgent assessment for sudden weakness or inability to raise the arm after injury, or new persistent numbness.

Change one variable at a time

When symptoms are too reactive, first reduce range, resistance, repetitions, or frequency. When the current dose feels controlled for several sessions, progress only one of those variables.

Progression

Build capacity in stages.

  1. Prepare the shoulder

    Continue comfortable cuff and shoulder-blade strengthening. Rehearse the step and turn without a ball. Before catch, your clinician should check your pain-free throwing motion, strength, endurance and control, and agree with you on a total throw count and distance.

  2. Repeat easy flat-ground catch

    Stay at the throw count and distance you set with your clinician and keep the effort easy. Count every warm-up throw. Leave at least one full day off throwing between early sessions. Repeat until you can throw pain-free and your arm feels and moves as usual the next morning.

  3. Build toward your position

    Agree with your clinician on one change at a time in throw count, distance, effort or days per week. A longer throw can take more effort. Stop if you have to force it. Before harder throws, have your clinician check your arm and trunk control at the current workload. Fielders can practice planned throws from their position before adding reactive plays.

  4. Rejoin practice, then competition

    Agree on a modified practice workload, then rehearse your full role in practice and check recovery the next day. Baseball pitchers need a separate clinician-guided flat-ground pitching, bullpen and simulated-game progression before competition. Decide on unrestricted play with your treating clinician and team.

Signs you are ready for the next stage

  • Daily use and sleep are comfortable, and you can use your usual throwing range without pain.
  • Your clinician has checked shoulder strength and endurance. Before you throw harder, they have also checked that you control your arm and trunk through repeated throws at the current workload.
  • You can transfer weight, turn and follow through without guarding or changing your arm action.
  • At your current distance and workload, repeated sessions are pain-free, with usual motion and comfort the next morning.
  • Before competition, you feel confident and have completed a full practice in your position with your usual comfort and motion the next morning. You, your treating clinician and your team agree on your return.

When to schedule an evaluation

Call 911 for chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting. Go to the emergency department now if your shoulder looks out of place or your hand is cold, pale or blue. A newly hot, swollen and severely painful shoulder also needs emergency department care now, even without fever or chills. Seek same-day urgent assessment for sudden weakness or inability to raise the arm after injury, or new persistent numbness. Contact your treating clinician before resuming if pain recurs, the arm gives way, or strength or motion declines.

Questions about this program

When can I start playing catch?

Start after you can move through your usual throwing motion without pain, everyday activity and sleep are comfortable, and your treating clinician has assessed your shoulder strength, endurance and control. Agree on your throw count and distance with your clinician before the first session.

Which throws should I count?

Count every throw, including warm-ups, casual catch, fielding drills and practice. Pitchers also count bullpen throws when they reach that stage of their separate plan. Record how far and how hard you threw along with the total.

What if my arm is sore after catch or the next morning?

If soreness lasts more than 1 hour after throwing or is present the next morning, take at least one full day off throwing. Once comfort and motion are back to usual, repeat the last comfortable workload. Contact your treating clinician if soreness recurs.

When can I pitch from a mound or play a game?

Baseball pitchers need a separate progression through flat-ground pitching, bullpen work and simulated games. Before competition, complete a full practice in your position and check that comfort and motion are usual the next morning. Decide on clearance to compete with your treating clinician and team. Completing this guide does not clear you to compete.
